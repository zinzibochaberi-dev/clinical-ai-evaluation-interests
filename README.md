# Clinical AI Evaluation Interests

My clinical interests and priorities in evaluating AI systems for safe healthcare use.

## About Me

I am a medical doctor based in Kenya, with clinical experience in paediatric emergency medicine and an MSc in Public Health from the London School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine.

My interests include clinical AI evaluation, patient safety, clinical reasoning, and the use of AI in resource-constrained healthcare settings. I have contributed to clinical AI evaluation work through OpenAI HealthBench and Penda Health's AI Consult project.

## Evaluation Priorities

### 1. Patient Safety

Identifying harmful recommendations, missed emergencies, and inappropriate management. A response can sound reasonable and still put a patient at risk, for example by failing to recognise danger signs or by delaying referral when urgent care is needed.

### 2. Clinical Accuracy and Reasoning

Evaluating whether responses are medically correct and complete, and whether the reasoning behind them holds up. This includes how clearly an AI system communicates uncertainty, and whether it recognises when information is missing or when a clinician should be involved.

### 3. Resource-Constrained Settings

Assessing whether recommendations are practical and appropriate where diagnostic tests, medications, and specialist services may be limited. Advice that assumes access to resources that are not available can be unhelpful or unsafe, so evaluation should reflect the conditions in which care is actually delivered.

## Contributing to Clinical AI Safety Evaluation

I am interested in contributing to AI safety evaluations that are grounded in real clinical practice. This includes designing realistic clinical scenarios, developing and applying grading criteria, reviewing model responses, and helping make sure evaluations reflect the needs of patients and health workers in a range of settings, including low- and middle-income countries.
